## long_distance_running_structure
Source: Seiler polarized training model; marathon training literature
URL: https://marathonhandbook.com/marathon-interval-training/
Use: Plan generation for endurance/long-run goals

Elite distance runners generally follow a polarized intensity distribution: roughly
80% of weekly volume at easy, conversational effort and about 20% at threshold pace
or faster. For marathon-specific blocks, the highest-yield sessions are long runs
with a portion at goal race pace (e.g. final 8-15km of a long run at marathon pace),
rather than short, very high-intensity intervals, which matter more for 5K/10K
preparation. Weekly long runs and 1-2 quality sessions (tempo or intervals) per week
is a common structure at recreational-to-competitive level.

## long_distance_progression_guardrail
Source: General endurance training guidance
URL: (compiled guideline — no single source)
Use: Plan generation safety ceiling

Total weekly running volume should generally increase gradually (a common rule of
thumb is no more than roughly 10% per week) to limit overuse injury risk. More than
2 high-intensity running sessions per week for recreational athletes increases
injury and overtraining risk without proportional fitness gains, since the ratio of
hard-to-easy running matters more than raw volume of hard sessions.

## sprint_training_structure
Source: Sprint training literature (acceleration/max-velocity periodization)
URL: https://www.1080motion.com/news/the-science-and-best-practice-of-training-elite-sprinters
Use: Plan generation for sprint/power goals

Sprint training is generally split into acceleration work (short distances, 10-30m,
focus on force application out of a low position) and maximal-velocity work (longer
flying sprints, 30-60m, at or near top speed). Strength training (squats, hinges,
step-ups) and plyometrics (bounds, jumps) support force production and are typically
scheduled a day apart from high-intensity sprint sessions to avoid compounding
fatigue. Full recovery between sprint reps (multiple minutes) is standard, since the
goal is quality of each rep, not conditioning volume.

## sprint_plyometric_guardrail
Source: Sprint training literature
URL: https://www.elitetrack.com/forums/topic/100200-sprinter-summer-training-program/
Use: Plan generation safety ceiling, exercise substitution

Plyometric and max-velocity sprint work carries higher injury risk (hamstring,
achilles) than general conditioning and should only be introduced once basic
strength and movement quality are established. Volume should stay low relative to
intensity — sets of single-digit reps with full recovery, not high-rep circuits —
and should be reduced or removed entirely on days with elevated soreness or
lower-limb pain flags.

## badminton_court_movement_demands
Source: Badminton conditioning literature
URL: https://www.strengthlog.com/strength-training-for-badminton/
Use: Plan generation for badminton goals

Badminton is characterized by repeated short, explosive multidirectional movements
(lunges, split-steps, direction changes) rather than sustained running, so
conditioning should emphasize agility, reactive footwork, and repeated-effort
anaerobic capacity over steady-state cardio alone. Common conditioning elements
include ladder/cone agility drills, shuttle-run interval training, and
lower-body plyometrics (box jumps, bounds) for explosive court coverage.

## badminton_injury_prone_areas
Source: Badminton conditioning literature
URL: https://www.continuaphysio.com/blog/badminton-specific-strength-and-conditioning-enhancing-your-game
Use: Exercise substitution, injury exclusion mapping

Badminton places repeated stress on the shoulder (overhead smashes), knees, and
ankles (lunges and rapid direction changes). Programs commonly include shoulder
stability and rotator-cuff work, single-leg strength and balance training, and
ankle stability drills specifically to offset these repeated-load areas, alongside
general strength and agility work.

## futsal_conditioning_profile
Source: Futsal physical-demands research
URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1728869X17301867
Use: Plan generation for futsal goals

Futsal is a high-intensity intermittent sport requiring repeated sprint ability,
rapid direction changes, and short recovery windows between efforts (high-intensity
bursts roughly every 20-30 seconds), relying heavily on the anaerobic/phosphagen
energy system rather than sustained aerobic running. Conditioning commonly combines
short sprint-interval work (e.g. 20-30s effort, 20-30s rest, repeated over
10-15 minutes) with agility and repeated-sprint-specific drills rather than long
steady-state cardio.

## futsal_strength_frequency
Source: Futsal strength & conditioning practice guidance
URL: (compiled from practitioner guidance — no single canonical source)
Use: Plan generation, weekly frequency defaults

Supplemental strength and neuromuscular training for futsal players is commonly
scheduled 1-2 sessions per week alongside regular futsal training/matches, at low
to moderate intensity, since match play itself already contributes significant
high-intensity load. Individualizing volume based on match/training schedule and
monitored fatigue (e.g. self-reported wellness) is a common practice among
strength and conditioning coaches in the sport.

## basketball_strength_power_structure
Source: Basketball athletic development guidance
URL: https://www.breakthroughbasketball.com/fitness/athletic-development
Use: Plan generation for basketball goals

Basketball strength training emphasizes explosiveness, lateral quickness, and
core/ankle stability over pure hypertrophy — combining compound lower-body lifts
(squats, deadlifts, split squats) with plyometrics (box jumps, bounds) to improve
vertical jump and change-of-direction speed. A common frequency pattern is 3-4
strength sessions per week in the off-season, reducing to 1-2 per week in-season
to maintain strength while prioritizing recovery for games.

## basketball_periodization_guardrail
Source: Basketball strength and conditioning guidance
URL: https://www.imgacademy.com/sport-camps/basketball-strength-and-conditioning
Use: Plan generation safety ceiling, seasonal adjustment

Basketball conditioning should progress from lower-intensity, higher-rep strength
and movement-quality work early in an off-season toward power- and plyometric-
focused training closer to the competitive season, since combining heavy strength
loading with high-volume plyometrics simultaneously increases injury risk without
proportional performance benefit. In-season programming should prioritize
maintenance and recovery over progressive overload given match/practice load.
